## Why

Clinical Schema Therapy has almost all the adult care functions: its Healthy Adult nurtures and protects Vulnerable Child, acknowledges Angry Child, allows Happy Child, confronts harmful Critic, sets limits, and practices behavior. These are **not novel inventions of this guide**. See:
- ISST, George Lockwood, `https://www.schematherapysociety.org/Limited-Reparenting`: therapist's limited reparenting is internalized into Healthy Adult, with imagery often used and early dependency sometimes welcomed.
- Cambridge Guide to Schema Therapy, `https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-guide-to-schema-therapy/intervention-strategies-for-schema-healing-1/5DE5F34AE83B28A4881B65E8DD64370D`: care, guidance, empathic confrontation, and limit-setting with later autonomy.
- APA, *Deliberate Practice in Schema Therapy* sample chapter `https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/deliberate-practice-schema-therapy-sample-chapter.pdf`: explicit objectives covering care for the child, adaptive coping, angry child, realistic limits, punitive critic, Happy Child, competence, and stage-dependent autonomy.
- Heath/Startup (eds.) 2020, all 20 chapters, demonstrates real variation among practitioners: some respect imagery refusal, others overinterpret refusal as defensive; somatic/play/couple/therapy endings covered.
- van Genderen (2026), eight chapters and eight appendices: exact practical adult behavior and learning; some lengthy diagnostic staging, with explicit real-world crisis exceptions.
- Capacchione (1991), 11 chapters: popular self-reparenting independently documented before Young's comprehensive 2003 manual; includes Nurturing and Protective Parents, play, spirituality and real boundaries.

The strongest **functional difference besides being self-directed** is not a special new set of psychological parts: it is the guide's **entry point, authority/consent rules, agency/real-world priority, developmental/spiritual aims and freedom not to accept particular therapeutic interpretations**. Which differences matter depends on the particular schema therapist, not the label alone. The guide might help readers screen for an unusually compatible therapist rather than assuming every schema therapist will match.
