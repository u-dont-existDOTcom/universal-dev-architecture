When a small, sponsor-associated intervention trial reports an unusually large, easily perceived improvement in physical performance, do not let its point estimate alone determine the practical best-choice ranking. Seek independent replication and adequately exposed, formulation-matched, outcome-specific firsthand user reports including no-benefit and adverse experiences. If evidence conflicts, classify the reported magnitude as isolated or discordant and reduce practical recommendation confidence accordingly. Keep the original published trial data in the evidence record, with source and methodology, rather than declaring it fraudulent or universally ineffective. A user may exclude particular products from their own shortlist even while the general scientific question remains open. Sparse reporting on a rare unmarketed plant is an underexposure state, not evidence of no effect.
