## What to do

When a user seeks images of **real, named plants** to recognize, purchase, or forage as medicinal material, retrieve species-verified botanical photographs from herbaria, botanical gardens, taxon-verified image collections or other traceable specimen sources. Prefer multiple diagnostic organs and record provenance, confirmed identification level, country/context and ambiguity. Label uncertain images and local lookalikes explicitly. Never treat AI-generated images as verified morphological evidence, and never encourage ingestion, harvesting, or buying from resemblance to a generated botanical plate alone.

If generated images are explicitly wanted as artistic illustrations, label them as illustrative/non-diagnostic; never present invented scale bars, species-specific inflorescences or explanatory morphology as authoritative unless independently checked. For medicinal plant procurement, ask for the actual sold material or natural-specimen photographs and, where needed, a qualified botanist/herbarium ID. Do not confuse this image sourcing requirement with a need to generate art.

## Why

A request for species images in a practical local medicinal-plant search received attractive AI-rendered identification plates. Generative renderings may combine true and false leaf, flower, fruit, bark, scale or plant-part details. This can create misplaced confidence when a local seller offers related or vernacular-name-ambiguous plants.

Check current AskRigor picture/media search behavior and existing image-authenticity and botanical identity instructions before adopting. Verify image origin and actual taxon coverage; do not add a mandatory photo gate to purely textual mechanistic research or a generalized shopping workflow.
