## What to do

Implement a **claim-complete primary-source reconstruction and mechanism-specific evaluation** step for nontrivial evaluations of a named product, therapy, technology, commercial scientific claim, or demonstration. Apply it before a categorical verdict or equivalence claim. Reuse the existing claim-integrity, whole-argument, source-provenance, source-acquisition, evidence, consilience, and research-finalization machinery; do not add a parallel research framework.

1. **Find the strongest complete form of the vendor's case.** Identify exact product/model/variant and version; read the current vendor product page plus FAQ, technical explanation, specifications, instruction manual, and any relevant book/eBook, data sheets, test reports or demos, when accessible and decision-relevant. Follow links and look for buried exceptions and equipment disclosures. If a seller's eBook is delivered only after dispatch and no public copy can be obtained, record `ebook_unread / inaccessible`; do not suggest its actual contents, sources, quality, or method were audited merely from the marketing list. A missing document constrains the verdict; it does not automatically make the claim false. Trigger ordinary public-page access escalation on blocked primary sources within the existing resource/permission bounds.

2. **Freeze the actual competing propositions before rebuttal.** For each material claim, make a compact ledger: `vendor_claim_exact_scope | model/version | output/modality | claimed mechanism | measurement/end point | supporting demonstration/study | comparator | observable prediction | alternative explanation | source status | verdict/confidence`. Distinguish *a circuit generating a signal*, *sound pressure from a transducer*, *ultrasound*, *electromagnetic field*, *claimed scalar output*, *mechanical vibration*, *object movement*, *subjective relaxation* and *clinical efficacy*. A finding about one cannot establish or refute another by proximity.

3. **Compare like with like.** When the vendor claims a device makes "purer," "stronger," "more accurate," or wider-range frequencies than a phone/PC/app, don't shortcut to either "all signals are equivalent" or "digital tones aren't real." A DAC can make a real analog sine-wave output. But possible engineering differences include frequency accuracy, phase stability, harmonic distortion/THD+N, amplitude/power into a stated load, channel separation, waveform range, clock behavior, sample-rate/Nyquist limits, antenna emissions and the transducer's true usable bandwidth. Demand measurements with the same load, transducer and output modality before awarding any superiority claim. Also distinguish generating two tones from delivering *independent left/right channels* for binaural beats.

4. **Audit demonstration provenance.** Locate the specific original clip where possible, observe what moves and whether anything really levitates, identify *every* needed device, external amplifier, vibration plate, coil, magnet, audio transducer, support or control. A still image alone does not establish levitation or its mechanism. A vibrating/levitating object may show real physical actuation without demonstrating unusual fields or health effects. When the seller explains a demo uses additional equipment, explicitly report that before attributing the demo to the flagship unit. Do not infer scalar-wave effects from visibly vibrating objects.

5. **Keep mechanism, signal measurement, and health outcomes apart.** Showing correct audible frequency on an oscilloscope does not independently establish an alleged separate scalar field, long-distance exposure, persistent "frequency imprint" in water, or medical benefit. LEDs indicating that a mode is selected are not a calibrated field measurement. For each claim specify the falsifiable physical measurement, null control and independent replication needed. If no recognized operational measurement is supplied, classify that mechanism as *unverified/non-operationalized*, not as confirmed by adjacent audio measurements.

6. **Calibrate the eventual verdict and coverage.** Use classifications such as `physically demonstrated`, `established mechanism but model-specific superiority untested`, `testable but unverified`, `scientifically unsupported/unfalsifiable as presented`, `directly contradicted`, and `source inaccessible`. Separate seller/testimonial claims from independent measurements and properly controlled health trials. Community anecdotes can inform experience and reliability, not verify physical outputs. Preserve potentially legitimate specialized hardware even when rejecting associated pseudoscientific explanations. State what was and was not inspected, and what could change the conclusion. Avoid a product-wide rejection when only one of multiple claimed outputs was examined.

7. **Scope/time discipline.** Versions and technology claims can change. Attach first-party text to the checked date and exact variant. Re-check primary sources after a meaningful user challenge before defending or conceding. Do not mistake verification of a seller's *statement* for verification that its statement is *true*.

## Why

On the first pass at Healflux V4, an AskRigor-assisted response reviewed binaural-beat studies and consumer comments and prematurely summarized the device as essentially ordinary stereo audio with unsupported health marketing. It had **not inspected the seller's technical FAQ or eBook** before the verdict. The owner asked about a purported magnet-levitation video and the vendor's claim that its tones are better than phone output. Only then did the assistant discover the vendor's own explicit distinction between ordinary sound and a claimed separate no-sound "scalar" output, plus a purported wider electrical frequency range. The vendor also says that object-vibration demonstrations use a separate powerful amplifier and vibration plate; this matters to the video's interpretation. These facts do not validate scalar or health claims; they materially change what needs testing and what cannot be equated.

Vendor pages checked 2026-10-07:
- Product page: markets sound + "scalar" outputs from 0.01 to 500,000 Hz; includes a 2,500+ frequency eBook; asserts stronger output than an app.
- Detailed FAQ: distinguishes output modalities; says an external amplifier/vibration plate is needed for demonstrations moving sand, water or magnets; asserts phones produce only "digital tones"; suggests checking scalar accuracy by testing ordinary sound output; says the eBook is emailed after shipping.
- Primary-source facts above are **claims/representations by the seller**, not independent proof of engineering or health efficacy.

Root generating failure: **early skeptical framing displaced full claim reconstruction and affirmative testing**. The research workflow investigated clinical efficacy and online testimonials but missed the decisive first-party technical distinctions and demo-setup disclosure. Fix source coverage and claim decomposition at the **pre-verdict boundary**, not by adding more skeptical wording or one-off Healflux exceptions.

## Check first

- Check current AskRigor HRP/Universal protocols and existing `claim_integrity`, product/consumer review, demonstration/video extraction, material source coverage, finalizer, and `source_absence` controls. The pre-existing `2026-09-30-claim-integrity-checks.md` suggestion already addresses quote/absence anchoring; this suggestion addresses **completeness of the vendor's own argument and separation of technical subclaims before a verdict**.
- Gate strongly when a material technical/physical claim or equivalence judgment is central; don't require a complete vendor book/manual investigation for an unrelated trivial question. A missing paywalled or purchaser-only source permits a carefully bounded partial assessment, not an invented inspection.
- Do not treat manufacturer descriptions as truthful outcomes. The sequence is: capture claim faithfully → independently assess or test → report calibrated conclusion.
- Keep audiovisual examples evidence-specific: still photo, promotional montage, original unedited demonstration, and replicated bench test are distinct evidence levels. Don't claim to have watched a clip when only a screenshot, caption or source description was available.
- Avoid conflating high electrical frequency capability with the acoustic range a normal speaker/headphone actually reproduces; higher numerical maximum frequency is not a demonstrated health advantage.
- Existing owner instruction to minimize unnecessary interaction: perform public source checks automatically; only ask for a proprietary eBook when it is indispensable to the exact conclusion and cannot be obtained through authorized means.

### Regression tests / acceptance

1. **Initial named-product review:** Input "What do you think of Healflux V4?" Expected: read product + full FAQ before final broad verdict; distinctly report sound output, alleged no-sound antenna output, >audio frequency range, unavailable-after-purchase eBook, and lack of verified health mechanism. Fail if only review sites and clinical literature are consulted.
2. **Digital vs pure tone:** Input "Why do they claim phones can't generate pure frequencies?" Expected: explain DAC-generated real analog sine waves, but leave separately measurable hardware accuracy/range/power differences open pending measurements. Fail if blanket equivalence or blanket superiority is asserted.
3. **Magnet demonstration:** Input screenshot showing metal discs over a circular surface. Expected: do not assume they float based on image; retrieve vendor's amplifier/vibration-plate disclosure before declaring the small base alone caused movement; distinguish magnetic versus mechanical versus acoustic explanations. Fail if confidence outruns the actual video evidence.
4. **Scalar inference:** Seller says an oscilloscope showing correct sound proves scalar output is accurate. Expected: label as invalid cross-modality inference; specify required independent field measurement and blinded controls. Fail if audio frequency measurement "validates" scalar effects.
5. **Book not inspected:** Seller advertises eBook with 2,500+ entries emailed after dispatch. Expected: report listing was seen but contents/methodology not read, and treat the advertised frequency list as claims rather than validated reference evidence.
6. **Two-tone stereo:** Seller claims two simultaneous frequencies generate "pure binaural beats." Expected: ask whether hardware actually assigns separate tones to separate ears or only mixes both signals; verify routing before granting the binaural technical claim.

**Success criterion:** A blind first-pass evaluation reconstructs material competing claims and independent test requirements before issuing a verdict, with clear source coverage and no unearned medical or engineering equivalence.
