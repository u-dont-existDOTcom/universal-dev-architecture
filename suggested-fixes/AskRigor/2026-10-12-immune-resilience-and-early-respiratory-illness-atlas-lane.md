Scope: **(A) prevention/exposure reduction**, **(B) earliest onset of respiratory symptoms (0–48h)**, **(C) established uncomplicated cold**, **(D) influenza**, **(E) fever/undifferentiated illness (triage first)**, **(F) convalescence**, **(G) susceptibility/immunocompromised groups separately**. Outcomes: laboratory-confirmed or clinically adjudicated infections, number of episodes/person-time, duration in days, symptom burden and fever, absenteeism, hospitalization, meaningful complications, patient-defined function and adverse events. Biomarker-only 'immune boosts' are secondary. Track mechanism/subgroup and drug standard-of-care without implying 'more inflammatory activity' is desirable.

**Baseline first:** sleep, ventilation/clean air, hand/contact exposure, appropriate vaccination and diagnosis-specific antivirals when indicated, hydration and nutrition, safe nasal saline spray/irrigation, ordinary rest and symptomatic care. Make **route** an identity dimension: nasal rinse/spray, oral, gargle, lung nebulization, steam and ambient aroma are NOT interchangeable. For undifferentiated fever in malaria-endemic areas, do not let herbal symptomatic care delay appropriate malaria testing or other urgent evaluation.

**First discovery/synthesis comparison classes and provisional evidence leads (not final rankings):**
1. Respiratory prophylaxis: 2025 network meta-analysis DOI 10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103479, 107 RCTs/101,751 adults, original review full text inspected, primary-study trials not individually audited: catechin vs placebo RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.66–0.95) overall RTI incidence; B. animalis RR 0.79 (0.63–0.99); multi-strain probiotics RR 0.90 (0.82–0.98). RTI duration catechin MD -2.64 days (CI -4.92 to -0.35; only two RCTs), probiotics MD -0.97 days (CI -1.78 to -0.16). Explicitly examine source formulations/doses, independent replication, small-study bias and clinical applicability. No extrapolation from one catechin product to green tea beverages or any brand.
2. Yeast beta-glucan: 2021 review DOI 10.1007/s00394-021-02566-4, abstract read only, 13 RCTs, pooled URTI incidence OR 0.345 (CI 0.192–0.620), authors note high heterogeneity; a separate Wellmune trial in 100 older adults gives OR 0.55 (CI 0.24–1.26) with 28 vs 17 medically confirmed URTI episodes and uncertain clinical significance. A 2025 broad prebiotic review describes several null yeast trials. Reconcile products, solubility, dose and bias before practical ranking.
3. Named consumer formula Natrium Health Immune Defense: manufacturer ingredient list **functional yeast extract standardized to 50% beta-glucans**, turkey tail 8:1, red reishi 16:1, black seed oil powder 5% thymoquinone, palmitoylethanolamide, oleamide, sucrosomial zinc, tyrosol. Original live product page inspected, only 2 reviews displayed; no clinical outcome trial of whole formula established. Ingredient RCT results do not prove a combo or establish that yeast is uniquely causal. Preserve exact ingredient/dose/batch specificity. Check credible mixed product reviews with denominator/context.
4. Early onset zinc: Cochrane 2024 34 studies, low-certainty therapeutic -2.37 days cold duration (95% CI -4.21 to -0.53; 8 trials, n972; I2=97%); side effects RR 1.34 non-serious (1.15–1.55); prophylaxis RR 0.93 (0.85–1.01). Route/forms matter: lozenge evidence cannot automatically transfer to swallowing low-dose zinc capsules. Intranasal zinc has special smell-loss concerns.
5. Vitamin C: Cochrane regular use duration about 8% less adults, 14% children; ordinary-population prophylaxis incidence RR 0.97 (0.94–1.00); large initiation doses after symptoms have no consistently established benefit. Need identify exact onset trials before extrapolation.
6. Saline **nasal** irrigation/gargling: 2019 randomized open-label pilot DOI 10.1038/s41598-018-37703-3, original full text inspected; illness duration 6.8 vs 8.7 days (~-1.9, CI -3.3 to -0.4), n61 analyzed. Important limitations: nonblinding and small sample, no matching placebo. Earlier Cochrane 2015 nasal evidence mostly uncertain. Compare Quinton-branded NASAL HEALTH sprays with sterile saline; **Quinton's PLASMA and DUPLASE glass ampoules are marketed as DRINKABLE**, not demonstrated/authorized lung-nebulizer formulations.
7. Oral supplements class: Pelargonium sidoides, Andrographis paniculata, echinacea by *exact* species/extract, elderberry, honey for symptom relief, and products with yeast-derived metabolites as separate discovery tracks, with independent trials and relevant community reports. Do not lead with DPPH/antioxidant assays or marketing.
8. Lifestyle: observational human viral-challenge sleep association (OR ~4.5 for <5h vs >7h sleepers; confidence interval wide) distinguished from randomized intervention outcomes; avoid claiming 'no sugar' or fasting has a proven causal prevention effect based on metabolic theory or anecdotes. Adequate hydration/nutrition during fever are priorities, particularly for vulnerable people.
9. **Safety, no unapproved airway aerosol**: Nebulized/aerosolized hydrogen peroxide should NOT be offered as a home treatment. CDC ATSDR documents airway irritation, inflammation and possible lung injury; dilution is not proof of safety. No established human clinical benefit against cold/flu that outweighs route-specific risk. Nasal irrigation, commercially sterile inhalation saline for a stated pulmonary indication, and sea-water oral supplements are distinct. CDC requires **sterile fluids designed for nebulization**, dispensed aseptically. Inhaling humic-acid powders or preparations has no established clinical respiratory-benefit evidence, is not established safe, and ingestion rodent safety data cannot authorize inhalation. Preserve reported experiences as hypotheses, without endorsing.
10. Fever in malaria-endemic context: prompt testing for suspected malaria rather than assuming viral URI; severe respiratory illness, dehydration or prolonged fever follow specific triage pathways. Acute influenza may have diagnosis-specific treatment with greatest benefit started early; herbs cannot displace indicated care.

**Research workflow:** establish exact population and symptom target -> baseline comparator/effect -> original evidence and full text/access status -> non-English ethno and specialist books -> primary citation ancestry -> product formulations, reviews and forums (benefit, nonresponse, harms) -> safety route and real-world salience -> adjust priority and provisional effect ranking -> rank missing original studies/books/chapters for user acquisition -> checkpoint diminishing returns and switch to better expected-value lane. Save a discovery log, source identity registry and inaccessibility queue; do not overproduce prose. Community reports can suggest efficacy but not incidence in self-selected populations. Search registered and published nulls; avoid false equivalence between no evidence and proven no effect.
