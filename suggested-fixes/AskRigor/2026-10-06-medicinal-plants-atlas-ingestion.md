## Research payload: overlooked medicinal plants

The ordering below preserves the earlier assistant's provisional judgment; it is not a validated comparative ranking and did not yet incorporate a systematic availability assessment.

1. **Galphimia glauca — standardized extract for generalized anxiety.** Prior synthesis reported a 152-person, four-week comparison with lorazepam and a separate 167-person, ten-week comparison with alprazolam, with anxiety improvement and less sleepiness/tolerability concerns than the drug comparators. No placebo arm; numerical between-group anxiety effects were not extracted. Different botanical populations may lack relevant galphimines. Do not substitute ornamental plants or homeopathic dilutions. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17562493/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4353648/ . Retrieve the second trial independently; its exact source was not preserved in the prior answer.

2. **Ageratina pichinchensis — standardized topical preparations for superficial fungal infections.** A six-month nail trial reportedly had 96 completers and mycological effectiveness of about 59% with extract versus 64% with ciclopirox, a crude difference of about -5 percentage points. A 160-person interdigital athlete's-foot trial reported cure near 34% and 42% for two extract strengths versus 40% for ketoconazole. These findings do not prove equivalence or apply to arbitrary powders. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18671197/ ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/acm.2011.0319 .

3. **Croton lechleri — source of crofelemer; separate preliminary wound-healing research.** In the ADVENT trial for noninfectious diarrhea in adults with HIV taking antiretroviral therapy, strict response was 17.6% versus 8.0% with placebo: absolute difference 9.6 percentage points, crude number needed to treat about 11 over the trial interval. Verify endpoint and duration. Crofelemer is a defined drug, not interchangeable with raw latex or products called dragon's blood from other species. A small skin-tag-removal wound-cream trial had attrition and multiple wounds per participant; it does not establish treatment of infected wounds or diabetic ulcers. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24334179/ ; https://mytesi.com/whatismytesi/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4737969/ .

4. **Senna alata — topical fungal skin disease.** Philippine medicinal-plant development includes a leaf-based antifungal lotion. A 20-person uncontrolled community-decoction study in tinea imbricata reported reduced itching in 95% but negative fungal microscopy in 40% after four weeks. These are uncontrolled response proportions, not causal effect sizes; symptom relief is not fungal cure. Sources: https://pchrd.dost.gov.ph/heartnovation/akapulko-lotion-as-antifungal/ ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/myc.13159 .

5. **Aloysia polystachya — anxiety symptoms.** A 54-person randomized placebo-controlled eight-week trial used powdered leaves, 300 mg twice daily, and reported greater Hamilton Anxiety Scale improvement. Numerical comparator-adjusted effect remains unextracted. Do not transfer this result to essential oil or infer immediate relief. A separate 2025 doctoral thesis with 36 adults with controlled hypertension provided limited 60-day safety observations, not broad long-term safety. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31279865/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037887411931983X ; https://repositorio.unaerp.br/items/2c738919-6953-4f6f-b05d-935b8b41aa5f .

6. **Quassia amara — 4% topical extract gel for facial seborrheic dermatitis.** A trial randomized 60 people, with 54 completing four weeks, against 2% ketoconazole gel and 1% ciclopiroxolamine gel. It reported a favorable result for Quassia, but the numerical between-group symptom effect was not extracted. This does not establish benefit from oral bitterwood or an improvised raw-powder gel. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23545914/ .

7. **Salacia reticulata — glucose-control adjunct.** A 136-person randomized placebo-controlled crossover trial in type 2 diabetes tested an extract-containing biscuit. Reported HbA1c advantage was approximately 0.25 percentage points, not a 25% reduction. No demonstrated prevention of diabetic complications; exact species, part, extraction and interaction with glucose-lowering medication matter. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37885536/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10599346/ .

8. **Sphaeralcea angustifolia — standardized 1% topical gel for hand osteoarthritis symptoms.** A four-week trial randomized 130 participants, with 113 analyzed, against 2% diclofenac gel. Reported improvement was approximately 89% versus 91%, a crude difference of -2 percentage points. No placebo arm; similarity does not establish equivalence. No evidence here of cartilage regeneration. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23528365/ .

9. **Clinacanthus nutans — topical shingles lesion-healing lead.** A 51-person placebo-controlled trial reported faster crusting/healing and pain reduction; numerical effects not extracted. Does not establish prevention of postherpetic neuralgia or viral eradication. Must not delay timely antiviral assessment, especially for eye involvement. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8576675/ ; https://www.cdc.gov/shingles/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html .

10. **Justicia pectoralis — 5% syrup for uncomplicated cough symptoms.** A 114-child randomized placebo-controlled trial reported caregiver-assessed cough, nasal-symptom and sleep improvements over 48 hours. Numerical effects not extracted. Children with suspected pneumonia or asthma were excluded; this is not evidence for treatment of those diseases or a safe homemade pediatric dose. Source: https://academicjournals.org/journal/JMPR/article-full-text/AB942FC66354 .
