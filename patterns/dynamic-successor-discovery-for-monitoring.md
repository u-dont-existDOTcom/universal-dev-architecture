# Dynamic successor discovery for monitoring

## Status

Current universal pattern.

## Purpose

Prevent recurring monitors and research briefs from becoming exact-name watchlists
that miss the thing replacing the watched target.

The governing invariant is:

> Monitor the function and ecosystem around a target, not only the target's current name.

## Activation

Apply this pattern to recurring or repeated monitoring of fast-moving domains,
including health interventions, drugs/compounds, products, software, models,
vendors, regulations, standards, markets, and community practices.

It is especially important after a displacement trigger:
- scheduling, ban, restriction, recall, withdrawal, or access loss;
- supply disappearance, vendor exit, reformulation, renaming, or migration;
- a known target becoming less usable, more expensive, unavailable, or risky;
- community discussion shifting toward replacements or workarounds.
## Required successor/substitution scan

After a displacement trigger, do not satisfy the monitor with exact-name searches
for the original target. Add a bounded adjacent-entity discovery pass.

Search from at least four directions:

1. **Replacement language:** replacement, alternative, successor, analogue/analog,
   2.0, next generation, post-ban, legal alternative, new formula, reformulation,
   substitute, workaround, and "works like <old target>".
2. **Family/mechanism map:** close chemical, biological, technical, product, or
   architectural relatives from primary literature, patents, standards, source
   trees, or other authoritative family maps.
3. **Community migration:** the communities that used the old target, including
   newly appearing aliases, code names, unnamed products, and comparison threads.
4. **Supply/implementation leads:** vendor, maintainer, developer, or operator
   announcements as discovery leads only; independently verify consequential claims.

Do not require the successor's eventual canonical name to be known before
investigating it.

## Identity discipline

A nickname, marketing label, fork name, "2.0" label, or vendor label is not an
identity proof.

For each candidate preserve:
- canonical/claimed identity;
- aliases and marketing names;
- identity confidence and evidence;
- whether different products with the same nickname may be chemically or
  functionally different;
- whether community reports can actually be pooled.

Never merge evidence across ambiguous identities merely because users describe
them as replacements for the same target.
## Evidence and action

For each plausible successor, evaluate the evidence classes relevant to the task:
primary mechanism/architecture, controlled evidence, real-world/community signal,
adverse events/failure modes, discontinuation or rollback behavior, interactions,
legal/regulatory status, and practical availability.

A successor can be decision-relevant before formal literature exists when:
- multiple independent, specific reports converge;
- the old target's displacement creates immediate substitution pressure; and
- the signal could materially change safety, guidance, monitoring, or near-term action.

Keep seller/maintainer claims separate from independent reports. Preserve
conclusion-level deduplication across runs.

## Stop condition

The scan is decision-sufficient when:
- the materially plausible successor classes and aliases created by the
  displacement have been checked;
- identity ambiguity is recorded rather than silently resolved;
- no additional search formulation is materially changing the action/conclusion.

This is a bounded recall correction, not a requirement to enumerate every remote
analogue or speculative substitute.

## Anti-patterns

- Searching only the exact watched entity after it has been banned or removed.
- Treating "no new paper on X" as "nothing changed" while users moved to Y.
- Waiting for a successor's canonical name before searching replacement language.
- Treating a vendor's "2.0" label as chemical/product identity.
- Counting repeated posts by one person as independent replication.
- Ignoring successor safety because the original monitor was scoped to the old name.

## Origin evidence

Promoted on 2026-09-20 after a recurring SR-17018 safety/guide monitor failed
to surface the post-ban successor SR-15099 even though public community discussion
had already moved through "SR 2.0" / replacement language and explicitly identified
SR-15099. A replacement-language search recovered the successor without seeding the
eventual compound name. The transferable lesson is the discovery topology: a
displacement event can invalidate an exact-entity watchlist by causing the ecosystem
to rename, reformulate, or substitute the object being monitored.

## Limits

- Do not broaden a stable monitor without a displacement or adjacent-entity reason.
- Successor discovery increases recall, not evidentiary confidence.
- Regulatory similarity is not proof of identical legal treatment.
- Structural or product-family proximity is not proof of equivalent efficacy or safety.
