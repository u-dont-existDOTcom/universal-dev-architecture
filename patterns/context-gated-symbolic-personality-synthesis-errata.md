# Context-Gated Symbolic Personality Synthesis — Errata

This file is a normative correction to `patterns/context-gated-symbolic-personality-synthesis.md` and supersedes any conflicting wording there.

## Predictive timing is not an alternative to ordinary causation

The earlier calibration rule that said an astrological timing match receives only "residual credit" when a concrete learning event explains the behavioral change is incorrect.

Separate two questions:

1. **Causal mechanism:** What ordinary events and psychological processes produced the observed change? Examples include betrayal, practice, feedback, reinforcement learning, grief, opportunity, deliberate training, physiological change, social pressure, and changing incentives.
2. **Predictive performance:** Before the event history is inspected, did the predeclared symbolic timing model correctly predict the domain, direction, and approximate timing of the change?

These questions are orthogonal. A concrete event can be the proximal cause *and* the event through which a successful astrological prediction is instantiated. The existence of an ordinary causal pathway does not reduce predictive credit.

A timing claim should instead lose credit for predictive reasons such as:

- the prediction was made after the event was known;
- the predicted window was too broad or shifted after inspection;
- the predicted domain/direction was vague or wrong;
- similar predictions would apply to many neighboring periods;
- a declared competing timing model predicted the event equally well or better;
- the relevant trait/change clearly predates the proposed timing factor;
- the model required subtype/orb/technique switching after seeing the biography.

### Correct scoring rule

> Score causal adequacy and predictive accuracy on separate axes. Never subtract predictive credit merely because a normal causal explanation exists.

## Cognitive phase correction

Do not classify a person globally as a "serial reasoner" versus "receptive/incubative thinker" when both are normal phases of problem solving.

Hold information sufficiency and task stage constant, then test whether the symbolic model predicts:

- when the person switches from generation/incubation to evaluation/execution;
- what triggers the switch;
- which problem classes remain active during incubation;
- whether execution becomes more serial once a candidate solution exists.

The existence of both deliberate reasoning and incubation is not itself a discriminating personality result.
