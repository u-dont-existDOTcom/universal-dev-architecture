# Contextual Symbolic Prediction: Causality and Phase Controls

## Purpose

Add two corrections to symbolic-profile/timing evaluation:

1. A symbolic timing model can be evaluated as a **predictor of when ordinary causal processes occur** without being treated as an alternative causal mechanism.
2. Creative incubation and deliberate serial reasoning are often **task phases**, not mutually exclusive personality types.

This file supplements `patterns/context-gated-symbolic-personality-synthesis.md`.

## Prediction versus causal mechanism

When evaluating an astrological, Human Design, or other symbolic timing claim, keep two questions separate:

- **Predictive question:** Did the predeclared symbolic factor predict the timing, domain, direction, or intensity of an event better than chance/baseline alternatives?
- **Causal question:** What proximal mechanisms produced the event (learning, loss, social feedback, reinforcement, physiology, opportunity, deliberate practice, etc.)?

Do **not** reduce predictive credit merely because a normal causal explanation exists. A successful timing predictor should be scored on preregistered predictive accuracy. Conversely, a timing match does not establish that the symbolic factor caused the event.

Recommended record for each event:

- observed event and independently dated window;
- ordinary proximal causal chain;
- symbolic prediction made before inspecting the event;
- timing/domain match score;
- competing baseline predictions;
- causal status: untested / unsupported / separately supported.

## No residual-credit rule

Avoid formulations such as "astrology gets only residual credit because there were concrete reasons for the change." Concrete reasons and symbolic prediction are not mutually exclusive. Instead report:

- causal adequacy of ordinary mechanisms;
- predictive adequacy of the symbolic model;
- whether the symbolic model adds timing/domain information beyond ordinary baseline forecasting.

## Phase-switching cognition control

Do not infer a stable cognitive type from whether a person sometimes uses incubation and sometimes uses deliberate serial reasoning. Established creative-process models distinguish generative/incubative and exploratory/evaluative phases, and individuals can cycle between them.

A useful symbolic model must therefore predict one of the following with specificity:

- which task states trigger incubation versus serial work;
- switching thresholds;
- preferred sequence of phases;
- duration or persistence of unresolved questions;
- whether new evidence/tooling reactivates dormant questions;
- whether outputs require a later formalization/verification phase.

Bad test:

> Are you an intuitive/incubative thinker or a deliberate/serial thinker?

Better test:

> Holding task stage and information sufficiency constant, what conditions cause you to stop deliberate work and incubate, and what conditions cause you to return to serial verification/execution?

## Dormant unresolved-question phenotype

Distinguish:

- **continuous rumination:** the question remains chronically active;
- **recurrent unresolved attractor:** the question can be shelved for months/years but repeatedly reactivates, especially when new evidence, tools, or conceptual leverage appears;
- **ordinary curiosity:** interest disappears unless externally prompted.

For any symbolic claim about "returning" or "mental looping," score these phenotypes separately. Do not count intermittent reactivation as continuous rumination.

## Holdout commitment

For autobiographical calibration, prefer a hidden prediction commitment before asking the user neutral questions:

1. write exact directional predictions and scoring rules;
2. hash the prediction file with SHA-256;
3. expose only the hash before collecting answers;
4. after answers are frozen, reveal the prediction file and verify its hash;
5. score hits, misses, mixed cases, and contradictions without reinterpretive repair.

Do not include private birth data in a public repository merely to make the commitment auditable. A commitment hash is sufficient to prove that the prediction text existed in fixed form before the answers.
